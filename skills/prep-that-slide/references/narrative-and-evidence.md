# Narrative And Evidence

## Give The Reader A Route

Start from the decision or understanding the reader needs. Sort source material by relevance to that goal, not by the order in which the investigation happened. Keep findings and the recommendations they justify close enough that the connection is explicit.

A review might move from purpose to the current state, evidence, priorities, proposed work, measurement and decisions. A product story might move from user problem to mechanism, experience and adoption. Research might need a question, method, result and limitations. These are examples, not required outlines.

For a reader deck, a short paragraph explaining why a fact matters is often more useful than six unexplained bullets. For a talk, more detail may belong in notes. Use a short cover subtitle to establish authorship and purpose; use aligned labels for recipient, author or review date only when relevant. Repeating administrative details on every page wastes attention.

Give each page a clear claim or task. Replace vague headings such as "Insights" with the actual observation when it is supported. Avoid duplicate eyebrow and title text. State recommendations in concrete actions the recipient can understand. Define niche terms on first use; keep standard technical names where precision matters.

## Keep Claims Traceable

Maintain a lightweight evidence record in the editable source or existing working notes: claim, source location, date or period, scope, and relevant limit. It need not become a separate client document. Preserve supplied exact values and distinguish these categories:

| Category | Wording and proof |
| --- | --- |
| Observed | Name what was inspected or measured and where. A saved screenshot or response can substantiate it. |
| Interpreted | Explain the inference and alternative explanations that matter. |
| Recommended | Say what should change, with an example and the goal it serves. |
| Unverified | State what access, data or test would resolve it. Do not present a guess as a finding. |

Keep sources and material limitations readable near the associated claim. Use concise citations, source labels or an appendix according to the brief. A prohibition on clickable links does not require stripping source names and dates. Distinguish screenshot evidence, source-code inspection, report transcriptions and new test results. A prototype or local build is not evidence of production behavior.

Use actual test dates. A document issue date or an "as of" label does not replace the dates of later measurements. Never relabel observations to imply that work happened earlier. When tests cover different dates, pages or environments, make that distinction in the relevant content.

Check quantitative claims: denominator, unit, date window, filtering, attribution and population. Counts from different reports may not be comparable. Event clicks do not necessarily establish a completed transaction. An automated score is evidence of its named checks, not a guarantee of business results or overall quality. Avoid universal benchmarks when the audience or sample does not support them.

## Turn Findings Into Decisions

Connect each substantial recommendation to a user goal and the evidence or rationale behind it. For unclear technical fixes, give a plain example: the current behavior, the proposed behavior and the consequence. Do not manufacture defects to justify work. A sound foundation is also a useful finding.

If prioritization helps, define a small legend for expected value, effort and urgency. Explain that these are judgments unless measured. Mark optional work clearly. A priority label alone does not say whether an item belongs in the proposed package.

Only include hours, price or delivery promises when the task calls for them. Base estimates on known scope, reuse, inputs, validation, release and review effort; distinguish preliminary judgment from a fixed commitment. Do not copy numbers, rates, AI-productivity assumptions or free-work promises from another engagement. Check totals and avoid counting the same task twice.

For delivery plans, separate acceptance checks from later outcomes. Specify what can be verified at handover and how later results can be compared. Do not promise ranking, revenue or adoption gains merely because implementation checks pass. Ask for client decisions that actually block the work, rather than making the reader design the whole solution.

## Compress Without Distorting

Merge redundant claims, shorten lead-ins and group details by meaning. Keep the examples that make recommendations intelligible. Split a page or add an appendix before hiding qualifications in tiny type. Respect a page-count limit by prioritizing the central argument and making omissions explicit; do not silently drop contrary evidence.

Stop when the presentation supports the requested decision. The skill does not authorize another website audit, data collection campaign, meeting, external test submission or product redesign merely to strengthen a slide.
