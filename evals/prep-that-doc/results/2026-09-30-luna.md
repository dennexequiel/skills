# Prep That Doc Smaller-Model Evaluation

Both skill versions fail the critical preservation check in this sample. The updated version preserves an underspecified operational expectation in one of two runbook trials; the previous version preserves it in neither trial. The updated version has a slightly higher observable score, but these results do not establish reliable preservation or general superiority.

## Method

Sixteen fresh native Codex subagents request [GPT-6 Luna](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-6-luna): four fixtures, two skill versions, and two repetitions per fixture and version. Each trial receives the same task and input bytes for its fixture, a private copy of one skill snapshot, and a separate temporary workspace. Parent conversation history is not passed to workers. Runtime instructions and available tools remain inherited.

The previous snapshot comes from commit `cb82094358db54c4a1342c5a3c1829ee06351dec`. The updated snapshot is the uncommitted skill revision under review. Skill copies remain byte-identical to their frozen snapshots throughout the trials. The [measurement record](2026-09-30-luna.json) contains source hashes, the candidate diff, exact prompts, inputs, outputs, saved reports, grades, and independent artifact checks.

Model selection is explicit; reasoning effort inherits the parent setting without an override. The native subagent interface does not expose the resolved provider model, exact inherited reasoning level, token usage, host version, or elapsed time in this record. No cost or latency comparison is made.

The fixture set covers a narrow PR typo edit, an incident runbook and clean README, block spacing with protected code and a reference-style link, and an arithmetic review with an unavailable remote artifact. The runbook inputs come from the recorded preservation exercise. Other fixtures adapt the current behavioral scenarios. Export inspection, roast mode, and the complete 23-scenario suite are outside this run.

## Scoring

The rubric assigns 0 for failure, 1 for partial fulfillment, and 2 for fulfillment supported by artifacts or reports. An independent reviewer grades shuffled outcomes without version labels or skill snapshots. The root reviewer checks the actual files and reproduces the final scanner results after grading. Grades are not adjusted after version identities are revealed.

Twelve criteria that combine artifact results with unobservable internal actions are marked `not verified`. Four criteria that would require an extra summary in a short proposal are marked `not applicable`; a mixed audience does not make a summary mandatory. These exclusions apply equally to both versions. Each version has 64 observable points available.

These scores measure the observed artifacts and reports on this rubric. They are separate from instruction-coverage scores and are not whole-task success rates. Partial credit can produce a high average despite a critical loss of meaning. The critical failure must remain visible beside the score.

## Findings

Three outputs delete the sentence "The command should finish quickly" from an incident procedure and omit the unresolved duration from their author questions: previous runs `run-04` and `run-11`, and updated run `run-12`. Their reports classify the sentence as non-actionable prose. Its missing duration is an information gap; deleting the expectation does not resolve it.

Updated run `run-03` preserves every non-heading runbook line and reports both the missing duration and replication-lag recovery threshold. The resulting scanner still reports the vague quantity candidate. All three outputs that delete the sentence produce zero scanner candidates. This directly demonstrates why an empty scan cannot establish editorial quality.

The second spacing trial for each version leaves some adjacent headings, paragraphs, or lists without source separation. The updated trial also leaves prose directly after the closing fence. These are incomplete source-layout passes under this rubric, not evidence that the Markdown is invalid or that a destination renderer fails. Rendering is not exercised.

Both versions correctly evaluate 120 times 4 as 480 requests/s under a linear-scaling assumption, and 4 times $0.10 times 720 as $288/month. All four reports treat the local upload receipt as a reported success and leave remote contents and rendering unverified.

The numeric rubric misses some editorial weaknesses. One updated report criticizes the throughput claim as insufficiently qualified even though its source sentence begins "Assuming linear scaling". Only one of the four evidence reviews identifies the unexplained "N times mu" notation. One previous report makes the local review appear blocked on a real-world scaling test, although the requested local arithmetic and editorial work can be completed. These observations remain in the per-trial records.

## Verification And Limits

- **Mechanical:** Independent post-run scans reproduce the reported final candidate states. Snapshot hashes and exact input/output comparisons establish the recorded artifact changes. They do not prove that workers executed their own reported scans.
- **Editorial:** Blind review checks the observed changes and reports against the shared rubric. Operational meaning loss is a critical failure independent of the aggregate score.
- **Factual:** The fixture arithmetic is independently checked. Supplied measurements, prices, and scaling assumptions are fixture inputs, not verified real-world facts. Worker tool traces are unavailable, so internal actions and the absence of external calls remain unverified.
- **Rendering and delivery:** Not verified. The delivery scenario uses a supplied local receipt; it performs no real upload or export. No destination artifact is available for inspection.

Two repetitions on one requested model provide a useful failure probe, not a statistical reliability estimate. The evidence supports a focused follow-up on preserving vague operational expectations and avoiding false findings about qualifications already present in the source. Neither version satisfies the preservation requirement consistently in this sample.
